Cybersecurity Awareness & Threat Defense Guide

Recognize the psychological manipulation techniques behind modern cyber fraud and protect your digital identity.

Common Social Engineering Triggers

  • Artificial Urgency: Threatening immediate suspension ("Account closed within 2 hours").
  • Unsolicited Financial Windfalls: Unexpected lottery wins, inheritances, or stimulus funds.
  • Fear & Authority: Impersonating tax departments, law enforcement, or executive leadership.
  • Delivery Traps: Fake postal tracking notifications requesting small redelivery fees to capture card numbers.

URL Safety Checklist

  • HTTPS Does Not Guarantee Trust: Over 80% of phishing sites now use free SSL certificates.
  • Check Domain Hierarchy: paypal.com.verify-access.net belongs to verify-access.net, NOT PayPal.
  • Beware Homograph Attacks: Attackers substitute Cyrillic or special characters that look identical.
  • Direct Navigation: Always type the known official domain manually in your browser.

What To Do If You Interacted With a Phishing Message

  1. Change Credentials Immediately: Update your passwords from a known secure device and enable Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA).
  2. Freeze or Alert Financial Cards: Contact your banking institution's fraud hotline to freeze compromised cards.
  3. Report the Incident: Forward scam SMS messages to 7726 (SPAM) or report to consumer protection agencies (such as the FTC or national CERT).
  4. Run Endpoint Scans: If any file was downloaded or opened, run an offline antimalware scan immediately.
